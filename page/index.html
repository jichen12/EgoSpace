Abstract

Egocentric world models now generate realistic first-person interactions from head and hand actions, offering a promising data source for embodied learning. However, existing models either omit explicit 3D geometry or provide it without resolving its depth order with the hands, leaving the generator to place each hand relative to the observed geometry. When this placement fails, generated interactions drift from the commanded actions even though the videos look convincing. Our key insight is that, given the initial frame, the commanded actions already determine where each hand lies relative to the observed geometry, so this placement can be computed before generation. To this end, we present EgoSpace, which aligns head and hand actions with the initial-frame geometry in one metric 3D space anchored at the initial camera. Compositing the hands with this geometry by depth along the head trajectory turns these geometry-aligned actions into a single action video that conditions a pretrained video diffusion model, which can focus on how the interaction unfolds. A realistic video can still depict an interaction that a physical hand could not reproduce. We therefore introduce a physics-based verification that progresses from counterfactual hand edits to dexterous execution of the reconstructed interaction in simulation. On HOT3D and H2O, EgoSpace reduces hand MPJPE from 45.4 to 20.7 mm and raises simulated task success from 11.2% to 18.6% (26.7% for real videos) over EgoHOI with separate head and hand control. It also reduces failures to follow rerouted hand commands from 85.4% to 29.3%.

Geometry-Aligned Actions

Where each hand lies relative to the scene is decided before generation, not left to the generator.
① Metric scene anchoring. Depth Pro estimates metric depth for the initial frame; scene pixels, excluding the initial hands, are back-projected into a colored point cloud. The initial camera defines the world frame, in meters.
② Coupling viewpoint and articulation. The scene points and the commanded MANO hands are rendered together along the commanded head trajectory and composited by depth. The resulting unified 3D action video, with a mask for unobserved regions and hand areas, fixes depth order and occlusion without specifying any future object state.
③ Action branch. The frozen VAE encodes the action video; with camera Plücker rays and the mask, a trainable action branch injects zero-initialized residuals into the frozen Wan 2.1 diffusion transformer.
④ Causal object reference. Two rendered views and an initial-frame view of the manipulated object enter as clean tokens outside the video's space-time grid (TSS-RoPE) through one-way attention, supplying identity while the object's motion must come from the actions.

Physics-Based Verification

A realistic video can still depict an interaction that a physical hand could not reproduce.
We test generated interactions at three levels: whether object responses follow edited hand actions, whether the depicted contacts produce plausible loads, and whether the reconstructed interaction can be executed by dexterous hands in simulation.

(1) Counterfactual Action Following

The first frame, caption, camera trajectory and object references stay fixed; only the hand command is edited. Withdraw: the hand halts at least 5 cm from the object without closing the fingers, so the object must stay. Reroute: after lift-off the wrist is translated 15 cm sideways over 15 frames, so the object must follow the hand.
Left: the edited action in 3D, with the initial-frame point cloud, the head camera and the edited hands; the live hand–object distance shows whether the edited hands ever touch the object. Right: the real video with the original action, and the EgoSpace and baseline rollouts generated from the edited action (inset, top-right), with whether each rollout follows the edit.

A violation is scored when, for three consecutive frames, the object moves without contact (withdraw) or lies farther than half the separation gap from the commanded hand (reroute). Object and camera trajectories are recovered from each rollout, and all thresholds are calibrated on real videos before any generated rollout is scored.

Hand IoU between generated and commanded hand silhouettes separates a failure to realize the hand command from a failure of the object to respond.

(2) Force and Moment Proxy from RGB

For each finger, the hand recovered by HaWoR gives a flexion share and fingertip penetration into the SAM 3 object mask, which define a contact load. Contact points and inward normals lie on the visible object surface reconstructed with Metric3D v2, giving a resultant force and a normalized moment. Joint balance rewards a resultant aligned against gravity with cancelling moments, temporal consistency penalizes abrupt load changes, and F+M fidelity compares the load system with the one estimated on the paired real video. These are scale-normalized geometric proxies, not calibrated forces.

Top: reconstructed interactions retargeted to dexterous hands in simulation; checks and crosses mark task success.

Bottom: RGB-derived fingertip contacts, net force and net moment. Checks and crosses indicate whether the generated net force agrees with the force estimated from the real video.

EgoSpace attains the highest joint balance (0.243), temporal consistency (0.557) and F+M fidelity (0.591) among generated methods.

(3) Dexterous Execution in Simulation

ViPE and Dyn-HaMR recover camera and MANO hands from each rollout and ProxyPose recovers the object trajectory; all states are aligned to the dataset frame using only the first frame. SPIDER then retargets the interaction to bimanual dexterous hands, which must move the object along the reconstructed trajectory through physical contact alone. Task success requires mean object errors within 10 cm and 0.5 rad.
Each example shows the EgoSpace rollout and its execution in simulation. The boxes in the simulation show the live contact-force map of each dexterous hand that touches the object, and the chart below gives each finger's share of that hand's contact force over time (grey: no contact). Visual Rank ≠ Physical Rank

Agreement with the real video's pixels and executability of the interaction in simulation are different properties of a rollout.

Uni3C exceeds EgoSim by 3.35 dB in PSNR, yet its Trans-AUC10 is lower (0.1453 vs. 0.1594). Generated Reality and EgoSim differ by only 0.21 dB in PSNR, while EgoSim leads by 0.0303 in Trans-AUC10.
